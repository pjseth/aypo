Feature one-pager

Wheat Procurement Desk

Everything a flour mill needs to price a cargo, on one page — read out of the email, WhatsApp and phone calls it currently hides in.

Built from a procurement walkthrough with Pavin Sethbhakdi, United Flour Mill, Thailand — 4 October 2026. UFM buys 10–12 Panamax cargoes a year from the US, Australia and Canada.

The problem

One price, scattered across six places

A wheat quote has four parts — futures, basis, freight and insurance, supplier margin — and a mill negotiates each of them separately. But the quotes arrive as prose in an email, as shorthand on WhatsApp, buried three levels into a forwarded chain, or as a LinkedIn message from someone who wants an introduction. Pavin already built a Power Automate flow to scrape his own inbox into a spreadsheet. That is the clearest signal there is: the work is worth automating, and it is costing him enough that he built the automation himself.

Email Formal offer tables, chatty prose, and forwarded chains where the number sits behind two levels of quoting. 185 of 288 messages
WhatsApp Shorthand from the trader's phone. No subject, no units, no sentence — basis +117 / fut 756.25. 55 of 288
LinkedIn Suppliers working a new relationship. Carries a price, never carries a futures reference. 31 of 288
Phone The real negotiation. Survives only as whatever got typed into a note afterwards. 17 of 288
Spreadsheet Futures positions, targets and P&L, in a workbook Pavin wrote himself. Most Thai mills keep it in their head. 1 file, 1 owner
Attachments Bills of lading, USDA phytosanitary, fumigation, halal — arriving against the thread, not the vessel. 143 documents, 9 per cargo
The feature

One page that leads with the decision, not the data

The page opens with the only number that answers "is this a good price?" — today's best offer against the window average for the same wheat class, converted into dollars per vessel, because that is the unit the desk thinks in. Underneath it: live Chicago and Kansas City settlements, the full forward curve, and the baht rate that decides what any of it costs in the currency the mill actually earns.

The mechanism

It reads the message, not a form

Nobody is going to make Cargill fill in a web form. So the desk takes the message exactly as it arrived and pulls the quote out of the text — the same job whether it is a formal offer sheet or six lines of WhatsApp shorthand at 10pm.

+$5.62/t The four stated components do not add up to the stated flat price. The desk flags the difference rather than burying it — $309,100 on this cargo, and a specific thing to put to the supplier instead of a general sense that the quote feels dear.

Across 288 messages the parser recovers 97% of the fields the desk needs, fully parses 80% of quotes with nothing missing, and correctly returns nothing at all for the 18 messages that carry no price — holiday notices, laycan chatter, market colour. Where it cannot read a field it says which one, rather than guessing. Every number carries a plausibility band, because the one error that would actually cost money is reading a freight figure as a flat price.

Why it compounds

Structure turns a price into an argument

Once every quote is split into its four parts, suppliers become comparable on the two lines that are actually negotiable. Futures and basis are the market and the same for everyone. Freight and margin are not.

The same structure runs underneath the rest of the page: market intelligence tagged by the driver it belongs to and by whether it raises or eases the mill's cost, and every certificate tracked against the vessel it belongs to rather than the email thread it arrived in.

Honesty

What is live and what is staged

LayerStateDetail
Futures, forward curve, FXLive Chicago SRW and KC HRW daily settlements and all six listed contract months, USD/THB, AUD/USD and crude as a bunker proxy — 1,279 price rows.
The quote parserLive Runs on message text alone, with no access to the values the test messages were written from. The parse path is the one real mail will take.
Supplier messagesStaged 288 synthetic messages across five formats and four channels, until a real mailbox is connected.
Market intelligenceStaged Notes and WASDE releases with the right shape. No live USDA pull yet.
Vessels and documentsStaged 16 cargoes and 143 certificates, with completeness tracked per vessel.

Two gaps are in the data, not the build: MGEX Minneapolis spring wheat has no free feed, so spring classes are priced against Chicago; and Western White has no futures contract at all — it trades on basis, and the page shows it that way rather than inventing a hedge instrument for it.

Next

What this does not do yet

Next up Futures position ledger. The page shows the market; it does not yet hold UFM's own contracts, targets and P&L. This is the spreadsheet Pavin maintains by hand, and the most direct thing to replace.
Next up Live WASDE parse. The release lands at midnight Bangkok time and the market moves within seconds. Pulling it on publication and summarising the bullish and bearish lines buys back the minutes Pavin currently stays up for.
Needs data Competitor benchmarking. The real KPI is buying below the other Thai mills, not buying cheap. Nobody publishes that; it needs either inference from flour bids or a swap between mills.
Not planned Supplier matching. Pavin was explicit that mills already have their relationships. A marketplace solves a problem he does not have.
